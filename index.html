Kitchen exhaust fire hazard

Grease, heat and a stopped fan — how kitchen fires start.

Cooking sends fat, oil and grease (FOG) into the exhaust. When any part of the system stops doing its job, that grease collects in the duct — and the duct becomes fuel.

01Exhaust fan switched off or brokenHeat can't escape from the hood and duct. A snapped belt or seized bearing is enough.
02ESP not workingThe electrostatic precipitator stops trapping grease and smoke, so it goes straight into the duct.
03Water-mist hood not workingNo wash cycle means grease bakes onto the hood plenum.
04Duct not cleaned on timeFOG builds up layer by layer inside the duct.
05Filters missing or cloggedGrease bypasses the filters, or airflow drops and heat stays in the kitchen.
What Sanco FM does

Monthly or quarterly KES technical visits, spare belts, bearings and motors on every van, ESP and water-mist checks — and a written fire safety notice the same day when a fire-risk item is found.
